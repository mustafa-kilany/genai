FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS - Jordanian Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) 2023

Q: What is the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)?
A: The PDPL is Jordan's comprehensive data protection legislation enacted in 2023. It regulates the collection, processing, storage, and transfer of personal data to protect individuals' privacy rights.

Q: Who does the PDPL apply to?
A: The PDPL applies to all entities that process personal data in Jordan, including government institutions, private companies, and organizations that handle personal information of Jordanian residents.

Q: What constitutes personal data under the PDPL?
A: Personal data includes any information that can identify an individual, such as name, ID number, email, phone number, location data, biometric data, and any other information that relates to an identified or identifiable person.

Q: What are the penalties for violating the PDPL?
A: Violations can result in fines ranging from 1,000 to 10,000 Jordanian Dinars, depending on the severity of the violation. Repeated violations may result in higher penalties.

Q: Do I need consent to process personal data?
A: Yes, in most cases you need explicit consent from the data subject. However, there are exceptions for legal obligations, public interest, or legitimate interests of the data controller.

Q: What are data subject rights under the PDPL?
A: Data subjects have the right to access their data, request corrections, object to processing, request deletion, and data portability, among other rights.

Q: How long can personal data be stored?
A: Personal data should only be stored for as long as necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, unless a longer retention period is required by law.

Q: Can personal data be transferred outside Jordan?
A: Transfers outside Jordan are permitted if the receiving country has adequate data protection laws, or if appropriate safeguards are in place, such as standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules.
